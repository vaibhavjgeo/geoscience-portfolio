§ About

I started as a field geologist - B.Sc. (Hons) Geology in India, mapping rocks and correlating aerosol loads with meteorology for my first thesis. At KIT's Institute of Applied Geosciences I found the questions I want to spend a career on: how the subsurface stores, moves, and yields heat, and how a changing climate rewrites those rules.

My M.Sc. thesis, supervised by PD Dr. Kathrin Menberg (Engineering Geology) and Dr. Susanne Benz (Geoinformatics, IPF), quantified climate-change impacts on closed-loop geothermal systems: finite-line-source theory, Brent's-method root finding for sustainable extraction rates, CMIP6 ensembles from Google Earth Engine, and uncertainty captured across model percentiles. Along the way I worked on geothermal and lithium-brine projects at the EnBW Bruchsal plant (Hydrosion GmbH) and produced research data and figures at KIT.

What sets my work apart is the delivery: I don't stop at the PDF. Models become interactive web maps, feasibility tools, and AI-assisted explainers that stakeholders can actually use - built with Python, GIS, and open data, hosted at €0/month. I'm open to geoscience, geospatial data science, and geoenergy roles - in industry or research, including PhD positions - where rigorous earth science meets modern data engineering.

PROJECT / 01

Climate x Geothermal Potential
M.Sc. thesis modelling framework

How much does climate change alter shallow geothermal yield? A Python framework couples 8 CMIP6 global climate models (SSP 2-4.5 & SSP 5-8.5, processed in Google Earth Engine) with finite-line-source BHE theory across Germany at 5 km resolution. Sustainable extraction rates solved per pixel with Brent's method; uncertainty captured via ensemble percentiles. Result: 1.7-3.1 °C mean ground warming boosts extraction efficiency ~8-24% by 2100 - each 1 °C substitutes ~4 m of drilling. Published as an interactive map portfolio.

"""Max sustainable heat extraction per grid cell:
largest q_l that keeps fluid temperature above T_min
over a 100-year horizon (finite-line-source model)."""
from scipy.optimize import brentq
import numpy as np

def solve_cell(years, params, t_min=-1.5):
    """Root of the fluid-temperature deficit function."""
    def deficit(q_l):
        # T_fluid(t) from MFLS + seasonal signal - resistance losses
        return fluid_temperature(q_l, years, params) - t_min

    # Brent's method: guaranteed convergence on a sign change
    return brentq(deficit, a=1.0, b=100.0, xtol=0.01)

# Sweep every 5 km pixel of the CMIP6-driven temperature field
grid = np.zeros_like(ground_temp_2100)
for (y, x), _ in np.ndenumerate(grid):
    grid[y, x] = solve_cell(100, params_at(y, x))
●EARTH ENGINE · CMIP6 SCENARIO EXTRACTION github → 
# Extract SSP-scenario ground temperature for Germany from
# a CMIP6 model ensemble and export as analysis-ready raster.
import ee
ee.Initialize()

germany = ee.FeatureCollection('USDOS/LSIB_SIMPLE/2017') \
    .filter(ee.Filter.eq('country_na', 'Germany'))

cmip6 = ee.ImageCollection('NASA/GDDP-CMIP6') \
    .filter(ee.Filter.eq('model', 'MPI-ESM1-2-HR')) \
    .filter(ee.Filter.eq('scenario', 'ssp585')) \
    .filterDate('2000-01-01', '2100-12-31') \
    .select('tas')  # near-surface air temperature

annual_mean = cmip6.mean().clip(germany)

ee.batch.Export.image.toDrive(
    image=annual_mean, description='ssp585_tas_mean',
    region=germany.geometry(), scale=5000,  # 5 km pixels
    crs='EPSG:4326', fileFormat='GeoTIFF').start()

Master's Thesis: Impact of Climate Change on the Geothermal Potential of Closed Systems Using GIS and Python

Institute of Applied Geosciences (AGW), KIT · PD Dr. K. Menberg & Dr. S. Benz
Python (NumPy / SciPy) Google Earth Engine CMIP6 / SSP QGIS / GeoTIFF folium / Leaflet

A climate-sensitive geothermal modelling framework for Germany - from raw climate rasters to per-pixel physics to a published interactive atlas.

  • Developed a semi-analytical heat-transport model in Python implementing moving finite-line-source (MFLS) theory (Rivera et al., 2017) with seasonal ground-surface signals and borehole thermal resistance (H = 150 m, λ = 2.5 W/m·K, Rb = 0.15 m·K/W); migrated the entire workflow from MATLAB to Python for automated climate-data integration
  • Engineered a geospatial data pipeline in Google Earth Engine processing 8 CMIP6 GCMs (BCC, CanESM, GFDL, GISS, HadGEM, IPSL, MIROC, MPI) under SSP 2-4.5 and SSP 5-8.5 to a harmonized 5 km EPSG:4326 grid, exported as analysis-ready GeoTIFFs
  • Solved maximum sustainable extraction rates per pixel with Brent's root-finding method (scipy.optimize) under the SIA 384/6 constraint (Tmin ≥ -1.5 °C) for 50-year, 100-year, and 100-year-sustainable operating horizons
  • Quantified climate-driven subsurface warming of +1.7 to +3.1 °C by 2100 and mean sustainable extraction of 26.97-47.39 W/m; showed sustainable 100-year operation yields 20-35% gains over depleting 50-year operation, with ensemble uncertainty captured across percentiles (mean, P25, P50, P75)
  • Published the results as 24 interactive folium/Leaflet map layers, a site-feasibility tool, and a grounded AI assistant (see Featured Work) - Publication: Jaiswal, V. (2025), KIT. doi:10.5281/zenodo.20540260
